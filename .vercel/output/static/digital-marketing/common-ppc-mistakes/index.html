Common PPC Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Common PPC Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

If you plan to include PPC in your marketing strategy, you need benchmarks to assess your campaign performance. The 2026 benchmarks, based on more than 13,000 US-based PPC ad campaigns, are as follows:

  • Average Cost Per Click (CPC) = $5.43
  • Average Cost Per Lead = $66.69
  • Average Conversion Rate = 8.18%

These figures vary by industry, but they provide a useful reference point for evaluating your PPC results. However, benchmarks alone do not explain why a campaign may underperform. Mistakes in keyword targeting, audience selection, ad copy, landing pages and conversion tracking can affect results and waste budget.

The good news is that many of these issues are avoidable. In this guide, you’ll learn about the most common PPC mistakes and how to avoid them.

Key Takeaways

  • Set clear PPC goals and track the conversions that matter to your business.
  • Target relevant keywords, search intent and audiences to avoid wasted clicks.
  • Make sure your ad copy and landing page match what users are looking for.
  • Manage your budget and bidding strategy around your campaign objectives.
  • Review performance regularly and optimise based on reliable conversion data.

 

What Are the Common PPC Mistakes and How to Avoid Them?

PPC is a powerful marketing tool that helps you reach an audience who are searching for what you offer. PPC ads appear at the top in search results when users look for relevant or exact search terms.


However, small mistakes can quickly lead to wasted leads, irrelevant traffic and needless expense. To avoid this, pay attention to common PPC mistakes.

Most PPC mistakes come down to a few key areas: planning, targeting, messaging, budgeting and measurement. You must understand these areas before you start your PPC campaign. This will help you avoid common problems and make better use of your time and budget. 

Mistake 1: Running PPC Campaigns Without Clear Goals

If you don’t have a clear goal before you start your PPC campaign, you won’t know if it’s working. Brands often take clicks on their ads as a measure of success, but that does not always translate into customers or revenue. 

For example, an online store would run a PPC campaign to generate sales. On the other hand, a local service brand may want enquiries or phone calls. This shows that each business has a distinct measure of success for its PPC campaign. Such a PPC mistake damages the results and efficiency of your campaign.
 

How To Avoid

To avoid such PPC mistakes, set one clear primary goal for your campaign and decide the metrics that will show the goal has been achieved. You could set the following goals and metrics to measure your campaigns:

  • Sales: Completed purchase
  • Lead generation: Form submissions or qualified enquiries
  • Calls: Phone calls from potential customers
  • Bookings: Completed appointments
  • Traffic: Relevant visits to your website

Once you have set your goals and metrics, choose clear KPIs that match them. This could include conversion rates, CPC, cost per lead or Return on Ad Spend (ROAS). When you avoid such PPC mistakes, you improve the ROI of your campaign and take measures to improve it as well. 

Mistake 2: Targeting the Wrong Keywords and Search Intent

A common PPC mistake that brands make happens when they choose keywords to use in their campaigns. Understand that the right keywords are not always the ones with a high search volume. Rather, your keywords should match what your audience actually searches for and what you offer. This means that your keywords should match the search intent of your audience. 

For example, a search for ‘cheap running shoes’ means the customer wants results for affordable shoes. Another search for ‘best running shoes’ has a completely different search intent. Notice that even though the two searches are about shoes, the intent behind them is very different. So, if your ad does not match the search intent, you end up paying for clicks that don’t convert. 

Another mistake is not using negative keywords. If you offer premium shoes, you should not appear for searches for ‘cheap running shoes.’ If you do, you only end up paying for irrelevant clicks and a non-converting audience. 

How To Avoid

To avoid this PPC mistake, find the keywords that best match the search intent and your offer. This will help your ad target the right audience instead of a broad segment. Once you have all the relevant keywords, then:

  • Group closely related keywords together
  • Choose suitable match types for each campaign
  • Review the Search Terms Report regularly
  • Add irrelevant searches as Negative Keywords
  • Remove keywords that bring poor-quality traffic

Your goal should be simple: pay for searches that are relevant to what you sell.

Mistake 3: Reaching the Wrong Audience

Even with the right keywords, your PPC campaign can still be ineffective if it reaches the wrong audience. If your ads reach the wrong audience, you may get traffic but fail to meet your goals.

When you target an audience, you decide who is eligible to see your ads. In a PPC campaign, this could be determined through factors such as location, demographics, devices, targeting settings and audience signals. 

For example, a local brand serving the audience in Manchester does not need to spend more to reach the entire UK audience. Similarly, a premium shoe brand would waste its PPC budget if it targets everyone who searches for shoes. 

How To Avoid

The best way to avoid this PPC mistake is to clearly define your audience before starting your campaign. To define your audience, you should consider:

  • Location: Where are your customers?
  • Demographics: Are there relevant age or demographic groups?
  • Devices: Where are your customers most likely to search or convert?
  • Audience segments: Are there specific groups with stronger buying intent?
  • Schedule: When are your customers most likely to take action?

Once you answer these questions, ensure that your audience aligns with your goals. This will help you review your performance by audience, location and devices. As a result, you will clearly know which aspect of your audience is giving you the best results. 

‘The aim of marketing is to know and understand the customer so well the product or service fits him and sells itself.’ Peter F. Drucker

Mistake 4: Writing Generic or Unconvincing Ad Copy

Another PPC mistake is to just get your ad in front of the right audience and hope it works. In reality, that is only half of the job. You also need to give your audience a reason to click on your ad. For this, you need to avoid generic and unconvincing ad copy. 

Generic ad copy such as ‘High-Quality Services at Great Prices’ gives your audience little to no information about why they should click on your ad. That is why you need to focus on what your ad copy tells your audience. It quickly tells the audience what you offer and why the offer is relevant to their search. 

How To Avoid

To avoid this common PPC mistake, write a unique ad copy centred on your customer’s needs. This is better than simply explaining your brand to the audience. To write the best ad copy, you should include:

  • The product or service being searched
  • A clear benefit
  • A useful differentiator
  • Relevant offers or information
  • A clear call to action

When you adopt this practice, you switch from: ‘Professional Accounting Services’ to ‘Small Business Accountants | Fixed Monthly Fees | Book Free Consultation’.

Also, ensure that your headlines and descriptions make sense when combined. Google recommends the use of multiple headlines and descriptions in Responsive Search Ads. This helps you test different combinations and see what works. With strong Ad copy, you can improve your clicks and conversions by 15%. 

Mistake 5: Sending PPC Traffic to the Wrong Landing Page

Even a relevant ad that targets the right audience with great ad copy may not perform. If that happens to you, you have made a common PPC mistake. This happens when your audience lands on the wrong page that doesn’t match the ad they saw. 

Imagine searching for ‘emergency boiler repair,’ and you find a relevant ad for it. Once you click on that ad, it takes you to a company’s homepage instead of the service it advertised. This will make you search through the website to find the service you needed. As a result, you abandon the search and leave the website. This is how you lose a potential customer and waste your PPC budget as well. Hence, your landing pages should be relevant, useful and easy to navigate for the audience. It should also match the intent with which a user clicks on your ad. 

How To Avoid

To ensure that you don’t make this PPC mistake, ensure that your ad leads to the page that best matches the search and the offer. For this, check that your landing page:

  • Matches the ad’s message
  • Provides the information promised in the ad
  • Has a clear next step
  • Works well on mobile devices
  • Loads quickly
  • Makes it easy to contact or buy from you

For example, if your ad promotes a specific service, your landing page should be that exact service instead of the homepage. This creates a clear journey for the audience from what they search, ads they see, pages they land on and the action they can take. As a result, you are less likely to lose a valuable click. 

Mistake 6: Mismanaging Your PPC Budget and Bids

A common PPC mistake is to assume that more budget leads to better results. Unfortunately, that is not the case. A large PPC budget does not automatically produce better results. Rather, even a big budget can become a big loss when it is poorly managed. This means you are either spending too much or too little on your PPC campaign.

Such mistakes happen when you set daily budgets without considering your target cost per lead or sale. Also, you make a mistake when you assume that using every channel would bring in more results. Hence, a poorly managed budget will be quickly drained without any useful outcome. 

Autobidding is another PPC mistake that brands make in their campaign. While it allocates your budget to your campaign, it doesn’t focus on your goal. As a result, you can end up spending your budget on campaigns that bring clicks but not the right results. 

Did You know: 

Google Ads accounts for approximately 70% of the search ad market.

 

How To Avoid

You need to first understand that not every channel is the right fit for your brand. So, pick a channel that works best for you, and start with a budget that you can afford to test and learn from. Once this step is done, you can:

  • Set a realistic target based on your campaign goal
  • Avoid spending more simply because clicks are rising
  • Give the campaign enough data before any major changes
  • Review cost per conversion, not just total spend
  • Use a bidding strategy that matches your objectives
  • Spend more on campaigns that produce the best result

If you do end up using Smart Bidding, opt for a strategy that best serves your goals and targets. Google offers strategies like Maximise Conversions, Maximise Conversion Value, Target CPA and Target ROAS. These strategies use Google AI to adjust bids to achieve your desired outcome. It was found that AI-optimised PPC campaigns improve ROI by 20%. Remember, your aim should not be to spend the most money. Rather, it should be to efficiently use your budget to produce valuable results. 

Mistake 7: Launching Campaigns Without Proper Tracking and Ongoing Optimisation

A PPC campaign can generate clicks and traffic, but fail to give you a clear picture of what those clicks or traffic are worth. This happens when you have poor PPC management and don’t track its performance. You may wonder why tracking matters when your campaign brings traffic. It matters because it helps you find gaps to improve and areas to focus. 

Without proper tracking, you may get the number of visitors on your website, but miss out on the crucial data. Without conversion data, you cannot see how many visitors became customers, submitted enquiries or completed purchases. As a result, you cannot use your budget efficiently. 

Also, the data you collect is not there just to tell you if your campaign works or not. It is supposed to help you optimise your campaign for better performance. It tells you areas to improve, pages to fix, keywords to add and budget to spend. 

How To Avoid

Set up your conversion tracking before the campaign is launched. This will help you track every aspect of your PPC campaign, which could include:

  • Purchases
  • Form submissions
  • Phone calls
  • Bookings
  • Quote requests
  • Sign-ups

Collecting such data is only the first part of this step. The next step is to use this data to optimise your campaign. For this, review your campaign regularly to find which keywords, ads, audiences and landing pages generate valuable results for you.

Your focus should be on patterns within your data instead of singular results. Look for patterns over time and optimise your campaign based on what the data says. This will allow you to focus on what works, reduce wasted budget and improve the performance of your campaign over time. So, your process should be:

 

Research ➡️ Launch ➡️ Track ➡️ Optimise ➡️ Repeat

Case Study: Optimised PPC Campaign for Better Results

Burleigh, an e-commerce brand, is a powerful example of how PPC campaigns can perform better when simple mistakes are avoided. It also shows how efficient use of budget can generate the best results for your PPC campaign.
 

The brand wanted to improve the profitability of its PPC campaign without increasing its budget. It wasn’t a small feat, but it wasn’t impossible either. So, the brand used data from its tracking to develop a new strategy and optimise its campaign. The new strategy focused on high-value repeat customers, new landing pages and optimised keywords for each product. The brand also allocated most of its budget to specific audience groups who had more buying potential. The results of these changes were what the brand aimed for.

According to the published case study, the changes resulted in:

  • 155% increase in conversion
  • 188% growth in revenue
  • 186% improvement in ROI
  • 61% drop in cost per acquisition

The case study teaches you a vital lesson about PPC campaigns: better performance does not always need a bigger budget. All you need to do is avoid common PPC mistakes to help you target better, be relevant and allocate your budget efficiently. As a result, your PPC campaign will work better and produce meaningful outcomes. 

Conclusion

PPC can be an effective way to reach potential customers and generate the right results. However, a successful campaign depends on more than just the budget you set for clicks. Due to simple mistakes, many brands end up wasting their entire budget. The most common PPC mistakes happen when campaigns begin without clear goals, relevant targeting, convincing ads, suitable landing pages, clear budget plans or reliable tracking. 

However, the good news is that these mistakes are easy to avoid. What you need to do is start with a clear goal, target the right audience, create relevant ads and direct those ads to useful landing pages. Once you have done all this, track the performance and optimise your campaign to achieve the best results. PPC experts ensure that each part of the PPC journey works together. As a result, your ad budget is used efficiently, and your PPC campaigns bring the outcome you want.

FAQs

What are the most common PPC mistakes?

Common mistakes include unclear goals, poor keyword targeting, weak ad copy, unsuitable landing pages, poor budget management and inadequate tracking.

 

How can I avoid wasting money on PPC?

Set clear goals, target relevant searches and audiences, use negative keywords, create relevant landing pages and track valuable conversions.

PPC mistakes can drain your budget very quickly. Overbidding, targeting the wrong audience, or paying for irrelevant clicks means you spend money without getting results. Fixing these issues helps your budget work smarter.

 

Why are negative keywords important in PPC?

Negative keywords prevent your ads from appearing for irrelevant searches, helping reduce wasted clicks and improve traffic quality.

You can avoid common PPC mistakes by doing proper keyword research, using negative keywords, writing clear ad copy, improving landing pages, and checking your campaigns regularly instead of setting and forgetting them.

 

How much should a business spend on PPC?

There is no fixed amount. Your budget should reflect your goals, expected conversion rate, customer value and acceptable cost per lead or sale.

 

How important is the landing page in PPC?

Very important. A landing page should match the ad and search intent, provide useful information and make the next action clear.

 

How often should PPC campaigns be optimised?

PPC campaigns should be reviewed regularly, but changes should be based on meaningful performance patterns rather than short-term fluctuations.
