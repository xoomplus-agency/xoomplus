What is the Difference Between On-Page and Off-Page SEO?

What is the Difference Between On-Page and Off-Page SEO?

Did you know that nearly 23.6% of eCommerce orders were driven through SEO traffic? This highlights the importance of SEO for businesses that want to attract customers through organic search. But ranking on Google involves more than adding keywords to a webpage. SEO includes both the changes you make to your website and the signals it earns from other websites and sources.
These efforts fall into two main categories: on-page SEO and off-page SEO. Both aim to improve a website’s visibility in search results, but they work in different ways. On-page SEO focuses on elements you can optimise directly on your website, while off-page SEO focuses on activities and signals outside your website. Understanding the difference between on-page and off-page SEO can help you choose the right optimisation tactics and build a more balanced SEO strategy. 
In this guide, you will learn how on-page and off-page SEO differ, what each one involves and how they work together to make your brand more visible. 

Key Takeaways

  • On-page SEO focuses on elements you can control directly on your website, such as content, headings, keywords, URLs and internal links.
  • Off-page SEO focuses on external signals that can build your website’s authority and reputation, including backlinks, reviews, mentions and digital PR.
  • The main difference between on-page vs off-page SEO is where the work happens: on your website or outside it.
  • On-page SEO helps establish a page’s relevance, while off-page SEO can strengthen its authority and external reputation.
  • On-page SEO can be changed directly, while many off-page signals need to be earned through useful content, relationships and recognition.
  • Neither approach should be treated as more important in every situation. A strong SEO strategy benefits from both.

On-Page vs Off-Page SEO: What Is the Difference?

On-page SEO involves optimising elements on your website, such as content, headings and page structure. Off-page SEO involves activities outside your website, such as earning backlinks, reviews and brand mentions. Although the aim of both on-page and off-page SEO is the same: improve a website’s visibility in search, they do this in different ways. The easiest way to understand their difference is to compare the role they play in improving your website’s visibility.

‘The difference between on-page SEO and off-page SEO is that they aim at two different sets of SEO factors.’

Mateusz Makosiewicz, Ahrefs’

1. Website vs External Signals

The first and most apparent difference between on-page and off-page SEO is the areas they focus on. On-page SEO focuses on the internal functions of your website. It includes the content you post, the structure you maintain and the experience you give to users. Whereas off-page SEO happens outside your website. This method uses external signals from other websites, platforms and people to improve your visibility.
Did You Know
In 2025, 42% of consumers said they trusted online reviews as much as they trusted personal recommendations.
For example, changing the content of your webpage is part of on-page SEO, while a review from a customer about your website is part of off-page SEO. Both of these differences ultimately lead to greater visibility and credibility of your website.

2. Relevance vs Authority

The practice of on-page SEO uses clear content, useful information and a well-organised structure. This helps search engines clearly understand what your webpage is about. As a result, it creates relevance between a user’s search and your website. Off-page SEO has an entirely different role. This SEO method uses external references to show that other sources recognise and value your website and its content.
Did You Know
Over 52% of keywords that people search for on search engines have informational intent.
To put it simply, on-page SEO establishes your website’s relevance, while off-page SEO builds your brand’s authority. While they differ in practice, on-page and off-page SEO help search engines rank websites at the top that are relevant and carry authority.

3. Content vs Backlinks

Content is the key element of on-page SEO. The content on your website should be clear and useful for people, and match their search intent. Strong content should have clear headings, relevant keywords and internal links. This makes search engines recommend your website, while viewers find your content useful. Whereas off-page SEO uses the same content to build backlinks. They occur when another website links to your website. However, the value of a backlink depends on relevance, quality and context. Relevant links from trusted websites can provide stronger signals than unrelated links.
This creates a clear distinction between the two methods. On-page SEO improves the content your website provides, while off-page SEO increases the recognition the content receives from outside.

4. Control vs Influence

Another key difference between on-page and off-page SEO is the level of control you have over the changes. With on-page SEO, you can rewrite content, improve internal links or enhance the website experience whenever you want. However, your control in off-page SEO is less direct. Instead of direct control, you rely on your influence and relationship with viewers and other websites to change your ranking.
What it means is that on-page SEO gives you more control over your website’s ranking, while off-page SEO relies on the responses of others to do the same.

5. Foundation vs Reputation

The key difference between On-Page vs Off-Page SEO is what they do. On-page SEO creates a strong foundation through useful content and a good user experience. Off-page SEO supports this foundation through external factors, such as mentions, promotions and reviews.
Think of it this way: on-page SEO lets you show your expertise on your website, while off-page SEO help others recognise that expertise. A strong foundation through on-page SEO makes external effort through off-page SEO more useful. This leads to higher rankings on search engines.

6. On-Site Trust vs External Trust

Building trust is the main motive of both on-page and off-page SEO. On-page SEO builds trust directly on the website through relevant content, transparent business details and evidence of expertise. Also, a professional and user-friendly website can build the trust of visitors and search engines. Off-page SEO builds trust beyond the website through external sources such as positive reviews, credible references and external promotions. This adds to the trust your website has as an expert.
Both on-page and off-page SEO support each other to build trust on your website. Where the former presents strong expertise, the latter provides external signals to support that claim.
These key differences should help you understand on-page SEO vs off-page SEO and make better decisions about your website.

What Is More Important: On-Page or Off-Page SEO?

It is hard to decide whether on-page or off-page SEO is more important. Their value depends on the strengths and needs of your website. In reality, both techniques should work side-by-side to make the best use of them. For example, a website with strong external recognition can still struggle to rank if its pages do not provide useful information. Similarly, a well-structured website may find it harder to rank if it has little recognition outside its own pages. This is why on-page SEO and off-page SEO should be used as a single strategy rather than competing options.
However, the stage of your website can shape your priority for on-page or off-page SEO. For a new or poorly optimised website, on-page SEO should be prioritised to make internal fixes before off-page SEO is used. This will ensure that visitors find structured pages and useful information on the website that answers their questions. While an established website can use off-page SEO to strengthen its position through external recognition. This helps a website build a stronger overall presence in search.
The key is to avoid treating on-page vs off-page SEO as a choice between two strategies. Instead, you should use a combination of both strategies based on the needs of your website. A balanced approach between on-page and off-page SEO supports the same goal: a website that is trusted by visitors and recognised by search engines.

Case Study: How Ahrefs Combined On-Page and Off-Page SEO

Ahrefs used both on-page and off-page SEO to improve their credibility, ranking and lead generation. To achieve this, the company created a new page focused on SEO statistics. The page used highly relevant content that provided useful insights, popular stats, additional data and clear categories for its visitors. This purpose-driven content appealed to the people searching for SEO data and drove considerable traffic to the page. Ahrefs then used off-page SEO to promote the page through external sources. This led to 36 editorial backlinks from 32 trusted websites. The case study reports that the page ranked first for the search term ‘SEO stats.’
This clearly shows the roles on-page and off-page SEO play in driving traffic, building credibility and ranking at the top. Through on-page SEO, Ahrefs used content and structure to bring value to the page. While the use of off-page SEO brought external links that helped increase the page’s visibility and authority. This shows how the two areas should not be treated as separate strategies, but should support one another to improve ranking and reach.

Conclusion

On-page and off-page SEO focus on different parts of the same goal: to improve a website’s presence in search. On-page SEO focuses on what happens directly on your website. It helps make your pages clear, useful and relevant to the people searching for information. Whereas off-page SEO focuses on what happens outside your website. It helps build recognition, credibility and authority through external sources.
Both these strategies play a key role in building your authority and relevance to visitors and search engines. Hence, they cannot be taken as a replacement for one another. The choice between the two should depend on what your website needs first. A strong website needs useful pages, which can then benefit more from external recognition.
Hence, both on-page and off-page SEO should be used together to make your website relevant to visitors and trusted by search engines. Once you understand the different functions of on-page and off-page SEO, you can give both areas the importance they need and build a more complete SEO strategy. The insights from an SEO expert can further help you make the best use of both these SEO strategies.
