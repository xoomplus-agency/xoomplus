Common SEO Mistakes That Hurt Your Website’s Performance

Elena Bostova, CPCAAPC Certified Professional Coder

Common SEO Mistakes That Hurt Your Website’s Performance

SEO mistakes are common and can affect how well your website performs in search. In a recent Ahrefs study, 95.2% of websites had 3XX redirect issues, while 72.3% had slow pages. These are just two examples that can affect a website’s SEO and user experience. Other mistakes can involve content, links, metadata, indexing or site structure. Some may seem minor, but together they can make it harder for search engines to crawl, understand and rank your pages. The good news is that most common SEO mistakes can be identified through a systematic audit and fixed with the right approach.  
This guide explains the common SEO mistakes that can hurt your website’s performance. It also shows simple ways to fix them.  

Key Takeaways

  • Choose relevant keywords based on search intent, not just search volume and make sure your content matches what users are looking for.
  • Create useful, well-structured and original content while avoiding duplicate content, keyword stuffing and unchecked AI-generated text.
  • Fix technical and mobile SEO issues such as broken pages, indexing problems, slow loading, poor navigation and Core Web Vitals issues.
  • Strengthen your website structure with useful internal links and keep your Google Business Profile accurate and updated for better local visibility.
  • Build trustworthy backlinks and regularly review your SEO performance.

SEO Mistakes That Hurt Your Website’s Performance

Several SEO mistakes can affect your website’s performance, from poor keyword research and content quality to technical issues. Here are some of the most common ones to watch for.

1. Keyword Research Mistakes

Keyword research helps you identify what people search for. A common mistake is choosing keywords only because they have high search volume. A keyword may get thousands of searches but still be a poor fit for your business. The search may have a different purpose from your page. For example, someone searching for ‘how to fix a leaking tap’ wants instructions. Someone searching for ‘plumber near me’ is more likely looking for a service. However, good keyword research considers:
  • What people want to know
  • The words they use
  • Search intent
  • How relevant the term is to your business
  • What type of page already ranks for the search
Google also advises website owners to think about the words users may search for while keeping the reader in mind.

2. Ignoring Search Intent

Choosing a relevant keyword is only the first step. Ignoring search intent is one of the common SEO mistakes that people often make. Your content must also satisfy the reason behind the search intent. Users may be looking for information, comparing options, finding a specific website or taking action.  
For example, someone searching for “best laptops for students” will likely expect a comparison of several suitable models rather than a page promoting a single laptop. If your content does not match that expectation, it may struggle to perform well.  
Therefore, before creating or updating a page, review the current search results for your target keyword. Look at the format, topics and level of detail used by the top-ranking pages. This can help you align your content with what searchers are looking for.

3. Duplicate Content

Duplicate content means the same or similar content appears on more than one URL. This can happen for several reasons, including:
  • Product filters
  • Printer-friendly pages
  • HTTP and HTTPS versions
  • URL parameters
  • Similar product pages
  • Copied content from other websites
Remember that duplicate content does not always mean a penalty. However, it can make it harder for search engines to decide which version should appear in search results. Google’s own case study of Saramin shows how removing duplicate content and using canonical URLs formed part of a wider SEO improvement process.

4. Poor Content Structure

A page can contain useful information but still be difficult to read. Long blocks of text make it harder for users to find relevant answers. Poor content structure can also make it harder to understand the main points of a page. For better content structure, you should use:
  • Clear headings
  • Short paragraphs
  • Bullet points
  • Tables where useful
  • Descriptive and logical headings
  • Simple sentences
In addition, your headings should follow a clear order. Each section should answer a specific question or cover one clear topic.

5. Keyword Stuffing

Keyword stuffing is also one of the most common SEO mistakes people often make. It means adding the same keyword too many times in an attempt to influence rankings. For example, writing ‘Our SEO service is the best SEO service for businesses because our SEO service provides reliable SEO solutions’. It makes the content unnatural. To avoid this, you should use your main keyword where it makes sense. Do not add a keyword forcefully into every paragraph.
Google also lists keyword stuffing as a form of spam. Its spam policies cover methods used to manipulate search rankings.

6. Low-Quality Content

Low-quality content does not give readers enough useful information. It may repeat information that is already available elsewhere without adding anything new. Common signs of low-quality content include:
  • Very little useful detail
  • No clear purpose
  • Unsupported claims
  • Poor research
  • Outdated information
  • Content written mainly to rank
  • Repeated information with little added value
To fix this common SEO mistake, you should ask whether your page gives the reader something useful that they could not easily get from a basic search. Add examples, original information, expert input, useful data or practical steps where appropriate.  

Google advises creators to focus on content that is useful to their intended audience and demonstrates real knowledge and expertise.

— John Mueller 

7. Overusing AI-Generated Content

AI tools can help you with research, ideas, outlines and editing. However, publishing large amounts of AI-generated text without checking or improving it can create problems. AI-generated content may contain:
  • Incorrect facts
  • Repeated ideas
  • Generic statements
  • Outdated information
  • Missing details
  • A lack of first-hand experience
The issue is not simply whether a tool was used. The key question is whether the final content is useful and original or not. To fix this, use AI as a support tool, not a replacement for human judgement. You should also check every important fact and add expert knowledge, original examples and useful insights.

8. Overlooking Technical SEO

Technical SEO helps search engines crawl, understand and index your site. Common technical SEO mistakes can include:
  • Broken internal links
  • Poor site structure
  • Indexing issues
  • Incorrect canonical tags
  • Incorrect redirects
  • Pages unintentionally blocked from crawling
  • Important resources blocked from search engine crawlers
These problems may stop important pages from being properly indexed. To fix this SEO mistake, you should run technical SEO checks on a regular basis. Use Google Search Console to look for indexing issues. Also crawl your website to find broken links and other issues.

9. Ignoring Mobile Optimisation

Many people browse your website using mobile phones. A website that works poorly on mobile can create a poor user experience and may affect SEO and search rankings. Common mobile optimisation issues include:
  • Small text
  • Difficult navigation
  • Buttons that are hard to tap
  • Content wider than the screen
  • Slow loading
  • Intrusive pop-ups
  • Images that do not fit the screen
To solve these issues, you should test important pages on different screen sizes. Check menus, forms, buttons, images and text. In addition, you should also check page speed and Core Web Vitals.
Pro Tip:
Do not check only your homepage. Test your most important landing pages, product pages and blog posts too.

10. Poor Internal Linking

Internal links help to connect pages on the same website. They help users move between related topics. They can also help search engines discover pages and understand how your website is organised. A common SEO mistake is creating pages without linking them to other relevant pages. For example, a blog about technical SEO could link naturally to related pages about:
  • Website audits
  • Mobile SEO
  • Page speed
  • Crawling
  • Indexing
The best practice is to review your important pages. Add relevant internal links where they help readers. Use clear anchor text that tells users what they will find after clicking.

11. Ignoring Local SEO (GBP Optimisation)

Local SEO helps your business appear when people search for products or services in a specific area. A common mistake is leaving your Google Business Profile (GBP) incomplete or outdated. You should add important information to your GBP profile. This includes:
  • Business name
  • Address
  • Phone number
  • Opening hours
  • Business category
  • Services
  • Photos
  • Website
  • Customer reviews
The best practice to make an effective local SEO strategy is to keep your business profile accurate. You should use the correct category, update your opening hours, add useful photos and respond to genuine customer reviews. For local businesses, ignoring this area can be one of the common SEO mistakes that limits visibility in local searches.

12. Forgetting About Backlinks or Building Bad Ones

Backlinks are links from other websites to your website. Relevant links from trusted websites can help people discover your content and can also support your site’s authority. Remember that not every backlink is useful. Buying links or using link schemes to manipulate rankings can violate Google’s spam policies. Poor link-building tactics can include:
  • Buying large numbers of links
  • Using spammy directories
  • Creating irrelevant links
  • Excessive link exchanges
  • Publishing low-value content mainly for links
To gain high-authority backlinks, you should create content that people want to reference. Digital PR, useful research and expert-led content can also attract natural links.

Case Study: How SEO Mistakes Affect Your Site and How to Fix Them

A useful UK example comes from a well-established property services company offering surveying, valuation and property management services. The business wanted to improve its visibility on Google and attract more visitors through organic search. In this process, an SEO audit identified several issues affecting its performance. These included broken links, poorly optimised meta tags and a local SEO strategy that was not targeting the right areas. The website also had opportunities to improve its keyword targeting and content. The SEO team carried out technical fixes, added more relevant search terms and improved key service pages. They also updated local landing pages to target more specific areas and used user behaviour data to improve the structure of the website.  
After applying all of the strategies, the results showed how fixing common SEO mistakes can improve search visibility. This includes:
  • 21% increase in overall website traffic year on year.
  • 125% increase in traffic to valuation and survey pages.
  • 143% increase in traffic to local surveyor landing pages.
  • 597 additional keyphrases appearing in search results.
This case study shows that SEO issues can be resolved with the right strategies. If you apply the right strategies, you can achieve your desired results.

Conclusion

SEO is not about making one change and waiting for rankings to improve. It is about improving the overall structure and content of your website. The common SEO mistakes covered in this guide include poor keyword research, ignoring search intent, duplicate content, keyword stuffing, weak content, poor technical SEO, mobile problems, weak internal links, poor local SEO and bad backlinks.
To avoid these issues, you should start with a simple process. First, understand what your audience wants. Then create useful content that answers their needs.
