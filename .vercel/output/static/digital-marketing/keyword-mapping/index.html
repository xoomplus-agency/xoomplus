What Is Keyword Mapping? A Step-by-Step Guide for Beginners

If you want to rank in SERPs and appear in search results, then you should know these two facts:

  1. 94.74% of keywords get 10 or fewer monthly searches
  2. Only 0.0008% of keywords get more than 100,000 searches per month

These facts clearly tell you why the right keywords are crucial for your SEO success. Keyword research is the first step for any SEO campaign. Keyword research gives you more than just the relevant keywords; it also tells you what people search for. However, finding keywords is only the first part of an SEO strategy. The next question is: ‘Which keyword should be on which page?’ This is where keyword mapping comes in. 

 

In simple terms, it is the process of assigning relevant keywords and keyword clusters to the right pages on your website. What makes this effective is that it gives each page a clear SEO focus and prevents creating several pages that target similar search intent. A well-built keyword map also guides content creation, on-page optimisation, internal linking and performance tracking. To sum it up, keyword mapping gives your SEO strategy a clear direction instead of a scattered plan for each page. 

In this guide, you’ll learn what keyword mapping is, why it matters and how to create it in a step-by-step manner.

 

What Is Keyword Mapping?

Keyword mapping is the process of assigning relevant keywords to specific pages on a website. The basic idea of this method is quite simple:

Keyword ➡️ Search Intent ➡️ Relevant Page ➡️ Target URL

Instead of trying to rank every page for every keyword, you create a clear strategy. This helps you decide which webpage can use the right keyword or keyword cluster in the best way. For example, a coffee shop may have the following keywords relevant to its brand.

  • Coffee beans
  • Best coffee beans
  • Coffee beans for espresso
  • Buy coffee beans online
  • How to brew coffee
  • French press coffee


Even though they are all relevant to the brand, you should not use them all on a single webpage. The keyword ‘buy coffee beans online’ belongs to a product page, while the ‘how to’ keyword belongs to an information page about the process. 

Hence, keyword mapping is more than just placing keywords into a spreadsheet. It is about aligning relevant search terms with the pages that best answer a reader’s intent. 

Keywords are like a compass for your SEO campaigns: They tell you where to go and whether or not you’re making progress.’ Brian Dean

 

Why Is Keyword Mapping Important for SEO?

Keyword mapping gives your SEO strategy a clear structure. What it means is that the same keyword is not targeted by several pages, while valuable topics are covered with dedicated pages. SEO keyword mapping creates a pattern of your research that helps you know which page should target which search term, and how those pages should be interlinked. 

Did You Know: 

Websites that are structured through keyword mapping see 60%+ higher organic click-through rates.

 

1. Target the Right Keywords

The success of your SEO strategy relies on the use of the right keyword on the right page. However, brands often choose a high search volume keyword, even if it does not match the page’s goal. This makes it difficult for the page to rank, as the content does not align with the intent readers visit it. This is where keyword mapping becomes important. It helps connect keywords to the page type to best answer search intent. 

This makes your SEO strategy more focused and clear. Instead of chasing high search volume keywords, you choose keywords that are relevant to your brand, realistic to target and right for the page. 

2. Prevents Keyword Cannibalisation

Keyword cannibalisation can greatly affect your SEO success. This happens when multiple pages use the same or similar keywords and target the same search intent. Take the example of a website that has three blog posts:

  • Best Running Shoes
  • Top Running Shoes
  • Best Running Shoes for Beginners

All three blogs target the same intent and cover nearly the same information, which makes them compete with each other for ranking. As a result, your website’s authority is split, and search engines are left confused about which page to rank. This is what keyword mapping addresses. It helps assign relevant keywords to specific search intent to use in a unique URL. A clear keyword map makes these overlaps easy to find before they become a larger SEO problem. 

3. Improves Search Intent Alignment

Keyword mapping helps SEO strategy align with the users’ search intent. For example, someone searching for ‘what is keyword mapping?’ wants an explanation. Whereas the search for ‘keyword mapping template’ wants a downloadable resource. Even though both searches are related, they do not need the same page. This is where keyword mapping helps you. It lets you group terms and searches with what the users really want. As a result, webpages align clearly with search intent, which makes your content more useful to readers. This ultimately leads to a higher ranking on search engines.

53% of UK small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) view SEO and keyword-driven search as their most effective marketing channel

4. Organised Content Strategy

The success of an SEO campaign relies on a clear content strategy. However, many brands often try to cover every new keyword without a clear plan or purpose. Keyword mapping addresses this flaw by creating a clear base for your content plan. For this, a strong keyword map can show you:

 

  • Which pages already exist
  • Which pages need optimisation
  • Which topics need new content
  • Which keywords are already covered
  • Which keywords are still unassigned
  • Which pages may overlap
  • Which topics should be connected through internal links

     

This practice helps create a more strategic content plan which builds upon defined topic areas and website goals. As a result, it forms the foundation for your topical authority and relevance. 

5. Supports Internal Linking

Keyword mapping helps create a more logical structure of internal links in your website. For example, suppose you have a main page about ‘SEO’ and supporting pages about keyword research, on-page SEO and link building. A keyword map explains how these pages are related to one another. This helps you create a relevant contextual link between every page. What this does is that it lets users find related content easily and gives search engines more context about the link between pages.
 

Also, Google highly recommends logical site structure and descriptive internal links to help search engines understand and rank your website. This makes keyword mapping crucial for your SEO success. 

6. Easier to Track Performance

Keyword mapping helps you measure SEO performance quite easily. This allows you to optimise and make changes to specific pages to improve your visibility and ranking. To do this, keyword mapping lets you ask useful questions:

 

  • Is the right page ranking for the right keyword?
  • Has the page improved its position?
  • Are impressions increasing?
  • Are clicks increasing?
  • Is another URL starting to rank for the same keyword?
  • Are new keywords appearing for the page?
  • Does the page still match the current search intent?

     

These in-depth questions let you analyse every aspect of a webpage and build the right strategy for success. Google Search Console lets you analyse queries, pages, clicks, impressions, CTR and average position. This makes it a useful data source for keyword mapping and a measure for your website’s performance. 

How to Do Keyword Mapping Step by Step

Creating a keyword map does not have to be complicated. To begin, you can use a spreadsheet and gradually add more details as your website grows. If you are a beginner or want to improve your SEO campaign, follow these seven steps to rank higher in search results. 

Step 1: Research and Collect Relevant Keywords

The first step is to build a list of keywords that are relevant to your website, audience and goals. For this, you should start with broad seed keywords. For example, if you are an SEO agency, you can start with:

 

  • SEO
  • Keyword Research
  • Technical SEO
  • Local SEO
  • On-Page SEO
  • Link Building

     

Once you have your broad keywords, you can expand these terms using keyword research tools to find different types of keywords, including: 

 

  • Short-tail keywords
  • Long-tail keywords
  • Question-based searches
  • Commercial keywords
  • Informational keywords
  • Product or service keywords
  • Location-based keywords

 

Did You Know: 

 

Long-tail keywords make up about 42.76% of UK search queries.

 

This research will give you a broad list of keywords that are relevant to your brand, audience and goals. However, do not use every keyword that you find. Your goal should be to create a useful list of keywords that meets your audience’s search intent and is relevant to your website. So, how can you know which keywords to choose? Step 2: Group Keywords by Topic and Search Intent

Once you have made a list of relevant keywords, the next step is to group them into related terms. This process is commonly referred to as keyword clustering. For example:

Cluster: Keyword Mapping

  • Keyword mapping
  • Keyword mapping SEO
  • SEO keyword mapping
  • Keyword mapping strategy
  • Keyword mapping process
  • Keyword mapping for beginners

These keywords can be grouped into a single cluster to be used on a single webpage, as they are closely related to each other. Such clusters are used to avoid creating separate pages for every small keyword variation. The real question you may wonder here is: how do you know if keywords belong on the same page?

The strongest test for this is to review the overlap between search intent and SERPs. You can search two keywords separately and compare the results search engines give for them. If the results are mostly similar for both searches, you can cover both keywords on the same page. 

Look at the next example for clarity. Consider two different situations.

Situation 1:

  • Keyword A: keyword mapping
  • Keyword B: SEO keyword mapping

If Google returns similar pages for both terms, they are close enough to be targeted with a single page. 

Situation 2:

  • Keyword A: keyword mapping
  • Keyword B: keyword mapping software

The first keyword generates results that are educational guides, while the second returns suggestions of tools and software pages.

This difference affirms that the two keywords are not the same and will need different pages.

 

Step 3: Analyse Search Intent and SERPs

After you have grouped your keywords into clusters, the next step will define what readers use these clusters for. For this, examine the actual search results on search engines. This is a critical step to create a strong keyword map. So, do not make guesses about search intent from the keywords alone. Rather, you should conduct thorough research to review the results for each keyword and assess the pages that rank. Here is what you should look for:

  • What types of pages rank?
  • Are they blog posts, category pages, product pages or service pages?
  • Are the results mostly guides or landing pages?
  • Are users looking for information, comparisons or products?
  • What topics do the ranking pages cover?
  • Are the results local?
  • Are there featured snippets, videos, forums or other SERP features?

The answer to these questions will give you clarity on what ranks and how it ranks. This often debunks your assumptions as well. You may assume two keywords belong together, but the search results would tell you otherwise. Hence, SERP analysis is crucial, as it gives you a reality check for your keyword map. 

 

Step 4: Assign Keywords to Each Page

Once the process of research is complete, the next step is to act upon it. In this step, you connect your keyword clusters to actual pages on your website. For this, start by reviewing your existing website against your keyword clusters. This will help you determine whether a relevant page already exists that meets the search intent. If it does, assign the keyword cluster to that page. If it doesn’t, decide whether you need to create a new page or not. This will make your keyword mapping template look something like this:

This practice will help you establish a clear relation between your keywords and URLs. Also, it helps prevent the common mistake of creating new content when an existing page can be improved. 

In addition, this stage also requires you to assign primary and secondary keywords to each page. The best practice is to have one clear primary keyword or primary topic for a page, supported by closely related secondary keywords. The primary keyword should represent the main topic of the page. The secondary keyword is meant to provide supporting context and cover related ways readers search for the same topic. This helps you draft useful, people-first content rather than stuffing the keywords throughout the page. Your goal should be to cover the topic naturally with the right keywords. 

Step 5: Optimise Your On-Page Content

After you have assigned your keywords to the pages, use your keyword map to help you in on-page optimisation. What you need to do in this stage is review your target page and ensure that your content genuinely addresses the assigned search intent. You can use the following checklist to review each page against the information on your keyword map:

 

  • Title tag
  • H1
  • H2 and H3 headings
  • Introduction
  • Main content
  • Image alt text
  • Internal links
  • URL
  • Meta description
  • Relevant supporting terms

     

A common mistake in this stage is to treat your keyword map as a list of phrases that must be included throughout the page. Instead, the right approach is to use that information as guidance for your content. For example, if the primary keyword is ‘keyword mapping’, your page should clearly explain the term. The secondary keywords should naturally appear in your content to satisfy other relevant searches. What you need to remember in this stage is that your content should serve the readers first. 

Step 6: Check for Keyword Cannibalisation

Before you begin your journey for higher ranking and authority, the last step is to review your website for overlapping pages and information. You should review each page to find multiple URLs that target:

 

  • The same primary keyword
  • Very similar keyword clusters
  • The same search intent
  • Nearly identical topics

     

Once you have your results for this review, evaluate the pages to find the ones that have similar content and intent. Such pages often compete against one another and prevent the focused page from performing the way it should. However, remember that multiple pages that rank for the same keyword should not be a cause for alarm. The example below explains this distinction clearly.

Consider two pages:

 

Page A: Best Accounting Software

Page B: Top Accounting Software

 

If both pages target the same audience, answer the same search intent and compete for the same search results, it is keyword cannibalisation. In another example, consider two other pages:

 

Page A: Best Accounting Software

Page B: Accounting Software for Small Businesses

 

While these pages may align to some degree, they can coexist if they have distinct audience, content and search intent. You can let such pages exist, but review them often to ensure they don’t overlap. However, pages that have similar content and intent require immediate action. The possible solutions for treating keyword cannibalisation include:

How to Avoid Keyword Cannibalisation

Strategy

What to Do

Consolidate similar pages

Combine two weak or overlapping pages into one stronger resource.

Redirect an outdated page

Redirect an old page with little unique value to a stronger, relevant page.

Reassign the keyword

Give each page a clear and distinct topic where appropriate.

Rewrite for a different intent

Adapt a page to match a different search intent, such as changing an informational article into a commercial comparison.

Improve internal linking

Use relevant contextual links to make the relationship between related pages clearer.

 

Step 7: Monitor and Update Your Keyword Map

Once you have completed all these steps, your webpages are ready to go live. However, the role of keyword mapping does not end here. This is not a one-time task, but requires regular checks and updates. It is likely that your website will change, new pages will be published, existing pages will be updated and even search results can change. These changes will require your keyword map to change and update as well. 

Semrush suggests reviewing your keyword map at least once every three months. You may need to review sooner if you update your website or content regularly. During this review, you need to focus on:

 

  • Keyword rankings
  • Organic clicks
  • Impressions
  • CTR
  • Ranking URLs
  • New keyword opportunities
  • Cannibalisation issues
  • Outdated content
  • New competitors
  • Changes in search intent

     

The best practice is to treat your keyword map as a working document that you can refer to, review and update regularly, rather than a spreadsheet created once and forgotten. Also, you can use various tools to help you create and update your keyword map.


What Are the Best Tools for Keyword Mapping?

Most brands assume that they need an expensive SEO platform to create a keyword map. The reality is different. The right tool does not depend on your budget, but on the size of your website, your goals and the amount of data you need to manage. The table below gives you a list of tools that you can use for keyword mapping. 

Tool

Purpose for Keyword Mapping

Google Search Console

Identifies the queries your existing pages already rank for and helps uncover new keyword opportunities.

Google Keyword Planner

Finds keyword ideas and estimates search demand, making it useful for building an initial keyword list.

Semrush

Researches keywords, analyses search intent and competition, groups related terms, and helps identify suitable target URLs.

Ahrefs

Finds keyword opportunities, analyses competitors and SERPs, tracks rankings, and identifies potential keyword overlap.

Moz Keyword Explorer

Provides keyword suggestions, difficulty estimates and prioritisation data for choosing target keywords.

Ubersuggest

Offers keyword ideas, search volume, difficulty and competitor insights, making it useful for beginners and smaller websites.

KWFinder

Helps find long-tail and lower-competition keywords that can be grouped into relevant keyword clusters.

Keywords Everywhere

Shows keyword data such as search volume, CPC and related terms directly while browsing search results.

AnswerThePublic

Finds question-based and related searches that can help expand keyword clusters and content ideas.

Google Sheets / Excel

Organises the final keyword map by connecting keywords with search intent, target URLs, rankings and content status.


Case Study: How Keyword Mapping Helped Improve the Ranking of CBD American Shaman

The right use of keyword mapping and its effect is evident in the example of CBD American Shaman. The healthcare company sold CBD oil and had a large product catalogue on its website. However, the brand’s old structure made it difficult for users and search engines to understand how products were organised. An analysis revealed that some of the product pages were trying to rank for broad terms such as ‘CBD oil’. This greatly affected the website’s ability to reach the right audience and rank in search results. 

 

The brand then restructured its website using keyword mapping to focus on relevant category pages. Instead of competing for broad category terms, each product page was grouped under relevant category pages. This led the keyword strategy to direct the website’s architecture, content and internal relationships. As a result, the brand witnessed a 13% spike in organic traffic within weeks. In addition, there was a visible improvement in the brand’s conversion rates. Over time, keyword mapping helped the brand double its organic traffic. 

 

This example clearly establishes that keyword mapping is not simply about adding keywords to existing pages. Rather, it should be used to direct a website’s content and structure. This leads to clear and useful content for the audience and improved visibility in search results. 

 

Conclusion

Keyword mapping is the direction for your SEO strategy. It helps you create a clear connection between search terms, search intent and webpages, instead of using random keywords. The process for keyword mapping begins with keyword research, which is then grouped into related terms.

Once this is done, you analyse the SERPs, assign keyword clusters to relevant URLs and optimise those pages around their topics. From there, keyword mapping helps you find content gaps, improve internal linking and spot keyword cannibalisation. As your website grows, review your keyword map to ensure it aligns with the changes and your goals. A regularly updated keyword map acts as a vital base for long-term SEO strategy. 

 

The effect of strong keyword mapping is more than just finding the right keyword. SEO experts know that strong keyword mapping leads to the creation of the right webpage to answer the right search.
